# Geopolitical Analysis

Explain international developments **from Malaysia's vantage point** with verified facts, multiple national perspectives and no great-power bias.

Always follow `CLAUDE.md`.

## When to use
- Malaysia's official foreign-policy positions and their changes.
- ASEAN matters (chairmanship, summits, Myanmar, ASEAN Economic Community).
- South China Sea / maritime boundaries / EEZ incidents.
- US–China competition and its effect on Malaysian trade, investment and security.
- Bilateral relations (incl. Singapore issues such as water, RTS Link, airspace, maritime boundaries; Indonesia; Philippines/Sabah claim; Thailand border).
- Trade agreements (RCEP, CPTPP), tariffs, export controls, FDI.
- Middle East and Malaysia's positions on Palestine/Gaza and related issues.

## When NOT to use
- Purely domestic politics → `political-analysis`.
- Primarily fiscal numbers → `public-finance-analysis` (use both for trade/FDI pieces).

## Core sources
- **Malaysia:** Ministry of Foreign Affairs / Wisma Putra (kln.gov.my) statements, PMO, MITI, Ministry of Defence, Defence White Paper, Parliament Hansard on foreign affairs.
- **Regional/International:** ASEAN Secretariat documents and chair's statements, UN records and votes, official statements of the other governments involved, international court/tribunal rulings, IMF/World Bank/WTO data.
- **Research:** ISIS Malaysia, ISEAS–Yusof Ishak Institute, RSIS, Lowy Institute, CSIS AMTI (maritime imagery), and others — note each institution's national base and funding.
- **Media:** Malaysian plus regional and international outlets from different countries (e.g. Malaysian, Singaporean, Indonesian, Chinese, US, Japanese, Middle Eastern sources) to avoid single-country framing.

No credentials or paid databases needed.

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Establish the facts
What happened, when, who said what — from official statements of **every** government involved, not just one side. Label and cite.

### Step 2 — Malaysia's interests
Identify relevant national interests: sovereignty and maritime rights, trade and investment, energy, security, ASEAN centrality, domestic political and public-opinion constraints, diaspora and religious solidarity considerations. Distinguish **official stated position** from **inferred interest** ([ANALYSIS]).

### Step 3 — Other actors' perspectives
For each relevant state/bloc, state its official position and its likely interests. Avoid framing any country as inherently good or bad. Use precise legal terms (EEZ, continental shelf, UNCLOS) correctly.

### Step 4 — Analyse
- Historical context with dates.
- Malaysia's traditional approach (e.g. non-alignment, quiet diplomacy, ASEAN-centred) — describe, do not idealise or dismiss.
- Economic exposure with numbers (trade share, FDI) from official sources.
- Options available to Malaysia and their trade-offs.
- Scenarios with indicators to watch.

### Step 5 — Bias check
- Have I relied mainly on one country's media? Add others.
- Am I treating one great power's statements as fact and the other's as propaganda? Apply the same standard.
- Have I presented contested sovereignty claims neutrally ("Malaysia claims…", "China claims…", tribunal ruling stated precisely)?

### Step 6 — Save
`analysis/geopolitical/YYYY-MM-DD-topic-slug.md` using `templates/analysis.md`.

## Output structure
```
# [Topic] — Geopolitical Analysis
As of: YYYY-MM-DD | Confidence: High/Medium/Low
## Bottom line for Malaysia (2–3 sentences)
## What happened (facts from all parties, cited)
## Malaysia's official position vs. inferred interests
## Other actors' positions
## Implications (security, economy, ASEAN, domestic politics)
## Malaysia's options & trade-offs
## Scenarios & indicators
## Uncertainties
## Sources (note country of origin)
```

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Official statements from all relevant governments cited.
- [ ] Sources from more than one country.
- [ ] Legal terms (UNCLOS etc.) used precisely.
- [ ] Stated positions separated from inferred interests.
- [ ] No great-power or ideological bias; symmetry test passed.
- [ ] Nothing published or sent externally.
