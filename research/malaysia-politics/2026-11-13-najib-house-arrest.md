# Najib Razak House Arrest — Research Brief

**As of:** 2026-11-13 | **Confidence:** High (core facts) / Medium (political reactions) | **Author:** Claude (draft) | **Reviewed by owner:** No

## Research question
On what legal basis is Najib Razak now serving his sentence under house arrest, what are the terms, and how have the main stakeholders responded?

Sub-questions:
1. What pardon was granted, when, and on what conditions?
2. Why was the transfer delayed between 6 and 10 November 2026?
3. What are the house-arrest conditions and how long do they last?
4. How does this relate to (a) the earlier "royal addendum" litigation and (b) his separate 1MDB sentence?
5. What positions have the government, parties and civil society taken?

## Key findings
- **[FACT]** On 10 Nov 2026, High Court Judge Datuk Aslam Zainuddin ordered Najib to serve the remainder of his reduced six-year SRC International sentence under house arrest at No. 11, Jalan Langgak Duta, Taman Duta, Kuala Lumpur, effective immediately, until 23 Aug 2028, returning to Kajang Prison on 24 Aug 2028 to serve his 1MDB sentence. [S-0001, S-0002]
- **[FACT]** The basis is a **conditional royal pardon** granted by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, Sultan Ibrahim, on 18 Sep 2026 under Article 42 of the Federal Constitution, conditional on payment of a RM50 million fine. [S-0004, S-0005]
- **[FACT]** The fine was paid on 6 Nov 2026 (reported as paid by UMNO from a donation drive). The Attorney-General's Chambers (AGC) then deferred the transfer, and the prosecution applied to replace the 6 Nov "Order of Discharge on Payment of Fine", arguing it could be read as discharging the whole SRC sentence. [S-0003, S-0005, S-0007]
- **[FACT]** Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail told the Dewan Rakyat on 12 Nov 2026 that Najib may not leave the address without written permission, may not attend public gatherings or take part in political activity, may not make media or social-media statements, may live only with specified family members, and may use only Prisons Department-registered devices. [S-0010, S-0011]
- **[FACT]** This pardon is **separate** from the "royal addendum" claim. The High Court ruled that addendum invalid on 22 Dec 2025, and Najib withdrew his appeal on 31 Mar 2026. [S-0012, S-0013]

## Legal / institutional framework
- **Article 42, Federal Constitution:** the Yang di-Pertuan Agong has the power of pardon for offences in the Federal Territories, exercised after advice from the Federal Territories Pardons Board. The Board includes the Federal Territories Minister and the Attorney-General. [S-0004, S-0012] *(Verify the article's text directly on lom.agc.gov.my before quoting it.)*
- **Distinction that matters:** the 22 Dec 2025 ruling invalidated the *addendum* because it bypassed the Pardons Board. The 18 Sep 2026 pardon was reported as decided at a Pardons Board meeting. [S-0005, S-0012]
- **Court's role (Nov 2026):** the High Court did not review the pardon itself. It replaced a standard-form discharge order with an order that sets out the pardon's terms and fixes the SRC end date. The 1MDB sentence then runs from 24 Aug 2028. [S-0001, S-0003]
- **Conditions:** issued by the Pardons Board. The Home Minister said they would serve as a template for future cases. [S-0010, S-0018 — template remark REPORTED]

**Steelman summary**
- **Supporters of the pardon** argue that clemency is a constitutional royal prerogative exercised through the proper Pardons Board process. The sentence has not been erased: it is still being served, only at home and under strict conditions. Najib also remains liable for the 1MDB sentence.
- **Critics** argue that the pardon is exercised on advice, so the government is accountable for it. Converting a corruption sentence to house arrest, they say, weakens public confidence in anti-corruption enforcement. On integrity grounds, they also question why a political party paid the fine.
