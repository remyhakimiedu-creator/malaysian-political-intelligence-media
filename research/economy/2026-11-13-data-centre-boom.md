## 1. Executive summary

**Bottom line.** [ANALYSIS] Malaysia is now one of Asia's biggest data-centre destinations. On paper, the money is very large. So far, though, much less of it has turned into buildings, jobs and value kept in Malaysia. The main risks to electricity and water are real but **mostly in the future and local**, not national today. The government has begun to tighten the rules, and its own officials now say incentives were too generous. What's missing is **public data** on how much tax was given up, how much each project really consumes, and how much value stays in Malaysia.

Key verified points:
- **[FACT]** MIDA approved **RM95.8 billion** of data-centre and cloud projects in 1H2026, close to **44%** of all approved investment (RM218.5 billion). [D-01]
- **[FACT]** The Data Centre Task Force has approved **27 projects worth an estimated RM225.97 billion**. As of the Digital Ministry's written reply to Parliament (Oct 2026), **only 1 was operating and 26 were under construction**. They are expected to create **4,053 jobs**. [D-02]
- **[FACT]** Bank Negara estimates the sector's **direct contribution at RM14.1 billion, about 1% of real GDP, in 2025 (forecast)**. Imports of specialised equipment are high, and profits will flow back abroad. [D-03]
- **[FACT]** Data centres used **7%** of peak electricity demand in 2026, and the government projects **up to 31% by 2035**. [D-04]
- **[REPORTED]** TNB has contracted **8.35 GW** for data centres, but actual load was **~1.26 GW in June 2026**. Much of the reserved capacity is unused. [D-05]
- **[FACT]** Approved water allocation is **445.04 MLD** for 2025–2030 (Selangor, Johor, Negeri Sembilan). Only **63.90 MLD (14.36%)** of that was approved for operating data centres by June 2026. [D-06]
- **[CLAIM]** Treasury Secretary-General Johan Mahmood Merican: "we perhaps overdid the red carpet for data centres." [D-07]

## 2. Five things Malaysians should understand

1. **Approved does not mean built, and reserved does not mean used.** Of RM225.97 billion approved by the Task Force, 1 of 27 projects was running. Data centres draw roughly a fifth of the power they have contracted. Headline numbers overstate what exists today. [D-02, D-05]
2. **Value kept in Malaysia is real but modest so far.** The direct contribution is about 1% of GDP. Most equipment is imported, and profits from foreign-owned facilities will largely go abroad. The jobs pay well (BNM: RM3,000–7,000 entry level, RM10,000–30,000 senior), but there are few of them. [D-03]
3. **The rules now say data centres pay their own way, but enforcement is the test.** Since 2025 there has been a separate high-voltage tariff, a separate water tariff and a "causer pays" principle for grid upgrades, and a 2027 tariff review is coming. Whether households stay insulated depends on how these are applied and disclosed. [D-08, D-09]
4. **Water risk is local and depends on the future.** Actual use is still small (e.g. 2.4 MLD average in KL and Selangor). If approved allocations are fully used, though, the volumes become significant, which is why Johor banned the most water-intensive tiers and pushes reclaimed water. [D-06, D-10, D-11]
5. **The biggest gap is missing information.** No public figure shows how much tax revenue incentives have cost, how much each data centre pays in tax, or how many jobs actually exist versus were projected. Without these, no one can answer "is it worth it?" with confidence.

### 3.2 Electricity and energy security
- **[FACT]** TNB supplied 36 operating data centres with a planned supply capacity of about 4.5 GW (Q1 2026). A further 23 under construction have demand met at 3.8 GW. [D-04]
- **[FACT]** Approved electricity supply capacity for data centres: **12.3 GW** (as of August 2026). Projected demand by 2030: **8,391 MW**. [D-08]
- **[REPORTED]** TNB's secured pipeline is 61 projects at 8.35 GW. Actual load was 1,256 MW in June 2026, up from 603 MW in June 2025 (TNB analyst briefing, 28 Aug 2026, via DC Market Insights). [D-05]
- **[ANALYSIS]** Actual load ÷ connected maximum demand = 1,256 ÷ 5,650 MW ≈ **22%**. This is indicative only: it uses one month's reading.
- **[FACT]** Peak national demand is projected to rise from 21.3 GW (2026) to 33.5 GW (2035). The data-centre share is projected to go from 7% to up to 31% (73,274 GWh). [D-04]
- **[FACT]** The government approved RM43 billion in TNB capex for 2025–2027 (RP4). [D-04]
- **[FACT]** Data centres must source at least 30% renewable energy (via CRESS or self-consumption solar). [D-08]
- **[REPORTED]** DayOne signed a 1.5 GW solar plus 2.2 GWh battery supply deal with TNB. [D-14]
- **Who pays:** see §3.5 and the impact assessment.

### 3.3 Water
- **[FACT]** Approved water use for data centres in Selangor, Johor and Negeri Sembilan for 2025–2030 is 445.04 MLD. Of that, 63.90 MLD (14.36%) was approved for **operating** centres as of June 2026. [D-08]
- **[FACT]** Average actual consumption in KL and Selangor was **2.4 MLD** from January 2025 to June 2026. The Digital Ministry says the reserve margin is "at a good level". [D-06]
- **[FACT]** A separate water tariff of RM5.30–5.38/m³ has applied to data centres since 1 Aug 2025 (Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan). [D-08]
- **[FACT]** SPAN's September 2025 guidelines prioritise reclaimed water, or air-cooled zero-discharge systems. [D-11]
- **[FACT]** BNM estimates a 50 MW data centre uses as much water per day as about 2,200 households, and as much electricity as about 22,000 households. [D-03]
- **[REPORTED]** Selangor projections: BIMB Securities projects 632 MLD of data-centre demand. Selangor's reserve margin was reported at 16.23% (Aug 2026), but the figures vary across reports. Air Selangor says data centres use less than they apply for. [D-15]
- Johor: see §4.

### 3.4 Employment and economic benefits
- **[FACT]** The 27 Task Force projects are expected to create 4,053 jobs. Of these, 3,523 are high-skilled, and 89.21% of the high-skilled roles are expected to go to Malaysians. These are **projections**. [D-02]
- **[FACT]** Minimum 30% local content is required for Task Force projects, monitored by MIDA every six months. TalentCorp is developing a facility-management programme. [D-02]
- **[FACT]** BNM salary ranges: entry-level RM3,000–7,000 and senior RM10,000–30,000, against a 2024 national median of RM2,793. Data centres "typically generate fewer jobs" than manufacturing. [D-03]
- **[FACT]** BNM: reliance on imported equipment "weakens data centres' backward linkages", which limits local vendors and technology transfer. Some suppliers (racks, cooling units) have begun manufacturing in Malaysia. [D-03]
- **[CLAIM]** Selangor exco Ng Sze Han: such projects "historically… have delivered limited spillover". [D-16]
- **[CLAIM]** Counterview (FMT columnist K. Kathirgugan): local contractors are winning hyperscale work, as in the Penang factory precedent. Example: Gamuda's RM1.72 billion contract. [D-17]

### 3.5 Government policy and accountability
- **[FACT]** The **Data Centre Task Force** was set up in February 2025, led by MITI and the Digital Ministry. It clears only projects with secured power and water and green compliance. [D-01, D-02, D-03]
- **[REPORTED]** On 24 Feb 2026, the PM told Parliament that applications for data centres not related to AI or high technology had been stopped. Full Hansard text not checked. [D-18]
- **[FACT]** The **Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill** is in its final drafting stage, targeted for the Dewan Rakyat in **2027**. [D-06]
- **[FACT]** Pricing measures: an Ultra High Voltage tariff for data centres above 50 kV from 1 Jul 2025, a higher Connected Load Charge, and the separate water tariff. Costs are allocated on the "cost causation" principle (DPM Fadillah Yusof, written reply). [D-08]
- **[CLAIM]** Johari Abdul Ghani (MITI), 15 Oct 2026: investors pay for the transmission upgrades they need, reserved capacity is billed in full, and there is to be no subsidy on data-centre power. Reported via The Star and DC Market Insights; legal text not published. [D-05]
- **[CLAIM]** Deputy Minister Abdul Rahman Mohamad (13 Oct 2026): the government is reviewing actual power and water needs to prevent "phantom demand", and "some data centres… aren't yielding many benefits". [D-19]
- **Incentives:** Malaysia Digital status (investment tax allowance on capex) and DESAC, which ties incentives to PUE/WUE efficiency benchmarks. Applications close by 31 Dec 2027. [D-03, D-20]
- **[UNVERIFIED]** **Tax revenue forgone** from these incentives: no public figure found.
- **Federal vs state:** federal bodies (MITI, MIDA, MDEC, the Digital Ministry, Petra/EC/TNB, SPAN) approve investment, incentives and power. States control land, planning and water operators (Ranhill SAJ, Air Selangor). Johor and Selangor have set their own conditions (§4). [D-10, D-16]

### 3.6 Political and geopolitical dimensions
- **[FACT]** ISEAS (James Chai, 2026): over US$34 billion from US and Chinese hyperscalers. Once colocation is counted, Chinese workloads "may rival or exceed American ones", leaving Malaysia hosting both US and Chinese infrastructure ("dual-stack"). [D-21]
- **[FACT]** Since July 2025, a MITI permit has been required to export, re-export, transship or transit US-origin high-performance AI chips. Importing them needs no permit. [D-22]
- **[FACT]** Nov 2026: MITI, with Customs, is checking whether chips imported by Speedmatrix (a Megaspeed unit) were re-exported, and is working with Singapore and US authorities. [D-22]
- **[FACT]** The JS-SEZ agreement was exchanged on 7 Jan 2025 and covers 11 sectors, including the digital economy. **Data centres are not named as a separate priority sector in the official sources checked.** [D-23]
- **[REPORTED]** Competition: Johor colocation vacancy is 0.7% versus Singapore 4.9% and Jakarta 20.5% (Knight Frank). Singapore limits new capacity. Batam offers SEZ tax holidays of up to 20 years and a planned 1.47 GW (BP Batam). [D-24, D-25]

### 3.7 Economic reality
- **[FACT]** BNM: the direct GDP contribution rose from RM0.9 billion (2021) to RM14.1 billion (2025f). Capital imports averaged RM17.8 billion (Jan–Sep 2025) versus RM10.7 billion (2022), pressuring the current account. ICT services exports rose 9% to RM16.8 billion (Jan–Sep 2025). [D-03]
- **[FACT]** BNM: as foreign-owned firms repatriate profits, "Malaysia's primary income account could face pressure". [D-03]
- **[ANALYSIS]** Task Force investment per projected job: RM225.97 billion ÷ 4,053 ≈ **RM55.8 million per direct job**. This excludes construction and indirect jobs. It reflects how capital-intensive the sector is, and it is not by itself a measure of value.
- Infrastructure cost vs tax revenue: **cannot be calculated from public data** (see the public-finance analysis).

## 4. Johor case study

**Why Johor.** It sits next to Singapore, which restricts new capacity, has cheaper land and power, and is close to submarine cables. [D-03, D-25]

| Indicator | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Live IT capacity | ~1,110 MW (1H2026), up from 94 MW in 2023 | Knight Frank; C&W [D-24] |
| Pipeline | 8,542 MW (Knight Frank) / 602 MW under construction plus 2,486 MW planned (C&W) | [D-24] |
| Colocation vacancy | 0.7% | [D-24] |
| Approved investment, all sectors | RM59.4 billion in 1H2026 (2nd after Selangor) | MIDA [D-01] |
| Task Force projects | 16 of 27 | [D-02] |
| Water operator capacity | Ranhill SAJ: 47 plants, 2,352 MLD; RM5 billion for 3 reservoirs and 3 plants | [D-11] |
| Reclaimed water to data centres | ~12 MLD (IWK and Johor Special Water); BDC 5 MLD (expanding to 20); Computility 4 MLD | [D-11, D-17] |

**State policy**
- **[FACT]** From 27 Nov 2025, Johor no longer approves Tier 1/2 data centres. The state says these can use up to 50 MLD, versus about 200,000 L/day for Tier 3/4. Tier 3/4 must be in industrial zones, use reclaimed water and sit away from housing, with five levels of PlanMalaysia vetting. Johor exco Mohd Jafni Md Shukor: "Digital growth cannot come at the cost of taps running dry for households." [D-10]

**Community impact**
- **[REPORTED]** In Feb 2026, Iskandar Puteri residents protested ZDATA's complex over water pressure drops and dust. **ZDATA says its site is not the cause.** [D-16]

**Geopolitical exposure**
- **[FACT]** The Speedmatrix re-export check (§3.6). [D-22]
- **[REPORTED]** Chinese-linked operators and tenants are significant in Johor colocation. [D-21, D-25]

**Assessment [ANALYSIS]**
- Johor is becoming a large **infrastructure host**. Whether it becomes a **technology hub** depends on local operators, engineering and supply-chain firms, and a skilled workforce. The evidence for those is still thin.
- The state's water rules are among the strictest in Malaysia, and enforcement data is the next test.

## 6. What the government should measure before approving more (summary)
See the political analysis. In short: actual versus reserved power and water per site, published quarterly; tax incentives cost per project; local content achieved versus the 30% required; actual jobs and wages; renewable share achieved; and ICT services exports and profit outflows.
