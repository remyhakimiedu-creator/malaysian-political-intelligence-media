# Data-Centre Boom — Electricity & Water Impact Assessment

**As of:** 2026-11-13 | **Confidence:** High (official allocations); Medium (actual use) | **Based on:** research/economy/2026-11-13-data-centre-boom.md

## Bottom line
[ANALYSIS] Today, data centres use far less power and water than they have reserved, and current household impact appears limited. The risk is about the **future**: approved and contracted volumes are large compared with national systems. Rules have been tightened, but households stay protected only if those rules are enforced and the data is published.

**Who pays [FACT/CLAIM]**
- Ultra High Voltage tariff for data centres above 50 kV, from 1 Jul 2025.
- Higher Connected Load Charge, to penalise over-reservation.
- "Cost causation": parties that create extra system costs pay for them (DPM Fadillah).
- Johari: investors fund the transmission upgrades they need, reserved capacity is billed in full, and there is no subsidy.
- A 2027 tariff review will focus on data centres ("user-pays").

**Households [REPORTED]**
- The Energy Commission reportedly said about 85% of households were protected after the July 2025 tariff restructuring.
- No published figure isolates the data-centre effect on household bills. [UNVERIFIED]

**Energy security & climate**
- [FACT] Data centres must source at least 30% renewable energy.
- [FACT] BNM warns that reliance on "brown electricity" could derail net-zero by 2050. [D-03]
- [ANALYSIS] More gas-fired capacity may be needed in the near term. Renewable deals (e.g. DayOne's 1.5 GW solar plus storage) help but have yet to be delivered.

445.04 MLD ÷ 1,160 litres per household per day (BNM's assumption) ≈ 383,700 households' daily use

**Safeguards [FACT]**
- Johor: no Tier 1/2 data centres; reclaimed water is mandatory for Tier 3/4.
- SPAN: reclaimed water or air cooling is prioritised.
- Separate water tariff of RM5.30–5.38/m³.
- Selangor: no approval without water and power capacity.

**Community risk**
- [REPORTED] Local complaints in Iskandar Puteri over water pressure (the operator disputes the link).
- Former SPAN chair Charles Santiago: "most of our data centres are using potable drinking water" (Jul 2026). This is a [CLAIM], and the share is not quantified.

## Overall assessment
| Risk | Current level | Future level (if approvals are fully used) | Main mitigant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Household tariffs | Low (protected by tariff design) | Medium | Causer-pays enforcement; 2027 review |
| Grid adequacy | Low–Medium | Medium–High | TNB capex; reservation penalties |
| Water supply, Selangor | Low (2.4 MLD actual) | Medium | Capacity-based approvals |
| Water supply, Johor | Medium (local disruptions) | Medium–High | Tier ban; reclaimed water; new reservoirs |
| Emissions | Medium | High | ≥30% renewable energy; CRESS |

[ANALYSIS] Risks are not overstated here. Current actual use is small, and the forward-looking risk rests on approvals that may not all be built.
