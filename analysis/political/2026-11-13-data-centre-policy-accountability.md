# Data-Centre Boom — Political & Government Policy Analysis

**As of:** 2026-11-13 | **Confidence:** Medium-High | **Based on:** research/economy/2026-11-13-data-centre-boom.md

## Bottom line
[ANALYSIS] Data-centre policy has moved from "attract at all costs" (2021–2024) to "attract, but on conditions" (2025–2026). The shift is driven by resource limits, local pushback and US chip scrutiny. Officials across government, at federal and state level, now say publicly that incentives were too broad. The main accountability gap is that the government sets the conditions but publishes little data showing whether they are met.

## Verified facts
- [FACT] The Data Centre Task Force (MITI + Digital Ministry) was set up in February 2025. It approves only projects with secured power and water and green compliance. [D-01, D-02]
- [FACT] Of the 27 Task Force-approved projects (RM225.97 billion), 1 was operating and 26 under construction. 84 projects had been approved nationwide by July 2026. [D-02]
- [REPORTED] PM, 24 Feb 2026: applications for non-AI and non-high-tech data centres stopped. [D-18]
- [FACT] The Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill is targeted for tabling in 2027. [D-06]
- [FACT] Tariff and water measures have been in place since July/August 2025, along with a minimum 30% renewable energy and 30% local content. [D-02, D-08]
- [CLAIM] Treasury Secretary-General, 24 Jul 2026: "we perhaps overdid the red carpet"; a "scorecard" will judge economic complexity, jobs and linkages. [D-07]

## Analysis by lens

**1. Actors & incentives**
- *Federal government:* wants investment headlines and an "AI nation by 2030" (MIDA's framing), but also has to keep electricity tariffs politically acceptable before GE16.
- *States:* Johor and Selangor gain land sales and investment but carry the water risk and local complaints. That explains Johor's tier ban and Selangor's "no capacity, no approval" rule.
- *TNB:* earns regulated returns on grid investment and uses take-or-pay contracts. It benefits from demand but carries the risk if reserved capacity goes unused.
- *Operators and hyperscalers:* want certainty on power, water and permits, and want to avoid US export-control exposure.

**2. Institutions & rules**
- Responsibility is split. Federal bodies handle investment, incentives, power and tariffs. States handle land, planning and water supply.
- The 2027 Bill is the first attempt at a single legal framework. Until then, rules are spread across tariffs, guidelines and state policies, which limits enforceability and transparency.

**3. Politics (all sides)**
- *Government MPs (PH)* have asked pointed questions about household protection and water: Syerleena Abdul Rashid, Onn Abu Bakar, Syahredzan Johan, Rodziah Ismail.
- *Opposition (PN)* has also scrutinised the sector: Tuan Ibrahim Tuan Man asked about power and water needs. [D-19]
- *Smaller parties and civil society* (PSM, Aliran, Sahabat Alam Malaysia) call for a pause or stricter conditions. [D-16, D-26]
- *Industry and some commentators* argue the backlash relies on worst-case figures. [D-17]
- [ANALYSIS] Concern crosses party lines. This is not a simple government-versus-opposition issue.

## Steelmanned perspectives
- **Pro-expansion:** AI infrastructure is a once-in-a-generation investment wave, and Malaysia has location advantages. Turning it away would send capital to Thailand or Indonesia. The new rules already make operators pay their own costs, and the jobs are high-paying.
- **Pro-restraint:** the projected jobs are few relative to the capital involved. Most equipment is imported and profits flow abroad. Resource risks fall on local communities. Without published data, the public cannot verify claims that households are protected.
- **Middle position (BNM, Treasury):** keep investing, but tie incentives to efficiency, local linkages and value-added jobs, and manage the resource externalities.

## What the government should measure before approving more projects
1. **Actual vs reserved electricity and water per project**, published at least quarterly, with clawback of unused reservations.
2. **Fiscal cost of incentives per project** (tax revenue forgone) against the expected taxes, wages and local purchases.
3. **Local content achieved** against the 30% requirement, from MIDA's six-monthly reports, aggregated and published.
4. **Actual jobs, wages and share of Malaysians** at operation stage, not projections.
5. **Renewable share achieved** against the ≥30% requirement.
6. **Net external impact:** capital imports and profit repatriation against ICT services exports (BNM already tracks parts of this).
7. **Compliance record** on chip re-export rules for tenants as well as operators.
