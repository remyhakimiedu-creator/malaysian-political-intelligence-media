# Data-Centre Boom — Geopolitical Analysis

**As of:** 2026-11-13 | **Confidence:** Medium-High | **Based on:** research/economy/2026-11-13-data-centre-boom.md; analysis/geopolitical/2026-11-13-nvidia-ceo-resignation-malaysia-chip-transit.md

## Bottom line for Malaysia
[ANALYSIS] Malaysia hosts both US and Chinese AI infrastructure. That suits its non-aligned approach, but it exposes the country to US export-control enforcement and to transshipment scandals. Johor competes with Singapore (constrained but richer) and Batam (incentive-heavy but infrastructure-poor). Its advantage rests on power and water, the same resources now under domestic pressure.

## What happened (facts from all parties)
- [FACT] ISEAS (James Chai, 2026): over US$34 billion from US and Chinese hyperscalers. Once colocation is counted, Chinese workloads "may rival or exceed American ones". Data centres have become "risk assets". [D-21]
- [FACT] Malaysia's July 2025 permit rule covers exports, re-exports, transshipment and transit of US-origin AI chips, under the Strategic Trade Act 2010. [D-22]
- [FACT] Nov 2026: MITI and Customs are checking whether chips imported by Speedmatrix were re-exported. MITI says it "never cleared" Megaspeed or Speedmatrix. Malaysia does not enforce other countries' unilateral export controls, but it expects firms to take them into account. [D-22]
- [FACT] The JS-SEZ agreement was exchanged on 7 Jan 2025 (Anwar and Lawrence Wong) and covers 11 sectors including the digital economy. [D-23]
- [REPORTED] Singapore's 2019–2022 moratorium pushed capacity to Johor. Singapore now allows limited green capacity. Batam offers tax holidays of up to 20 years and a planned 1.47 GW. Indonesia's total capacity is about 456 MW against Malaysia's 1.3 GW (Katadata-cited study). [D-24, D-25]

## Malaysia's official position vs inferred interests
| Stated position | Inferred interests [ANALYSIS] |
|---|---|
| "Malaysia welcomes investment in data centres and technology" (MITI); AI nation by 2030 (MIDA) | Capture AI-era FDI from both the US and China; avoid choosing sides |
| Compliance with own law; cooperation with Singapore and the US on safeguards | Avoid US secondary penalties or chip caps that would choke the sector |
| Approvals tied to power and water | Keep domestic resource politics stable |

## Other actors
- **United States:** tighter scrutiny of chip end-use, including the "remote access" loophole (see the Nvidia analysis). It is also the largest foreign investor in Malaysia (RM33.1 billion, 1H2026, all sectors). [D-01]
- **China:** major tenant and operator presence in colocation. It opposes US export controls.
- **Singapore:** a partner (JS-SEZ) and a competitor, with limited domestic capacity. Its firms are both investors and tenants in Johor.
- **Indonesia (Batam):** aggressive incentives, but water and power limits acknowledged by local authorities. [D-25]

## Implications
- **Economic security:** any US rule restricting compute rental to Chinese firms abroad would hit Johor colocation hardest.
- **ASEAN:** Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia compete on incentives. [ANALYSIS] There is a risk of a "race to the bottom" on tariffs and incentives unless the three coordinate.
- **Strategic value:** AI infrastructure gives Malaysia relevance with both great powers. It is also a lever others can use against it.

## Options & trade-offs
| Option | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| A. Dual-stack openness (status quo) | Maximum investment | Compliance and reputational risk |
| B. Stronger end-use tracking (planned law, disclosure) | Credibility with the US; protects the sector | Higher costs; may deter some tenants |
| C. Regional coordination (JS-SEZ standards) | Shared rules; less undercutting | Slow; sovereignty sensitivities |

## Scenarios & indicators
- **Tighter US rules on remote compute** (Medium): Senate action on the Remote Access Security Act; Commerce Department rules.
- **Malaysia passes the data-centre and chip-tracking law** (Medium–High): Bill tabled 2027; outcome of the MITI/Customs Speedmatrix check.
- **Investment shifts to Thailand or Batam** (Low–Medium): Savills/JLL inquiry trends; Batam capacity delivery.

## Uncertainties
- Precise US vs Chinese shares of capacity are not public.
- JS-SEZ data-centre-specific measures were not found in the official sources checked.
